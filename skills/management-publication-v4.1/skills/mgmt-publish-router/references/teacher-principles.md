# Advisor-derived research principles

These principles summarize the advisor discussion supplied for this skill suite.

1. A research problem must be rooted in an existing scholarly conversation, dispute, limitation, theory, or important new context.
2. A normal paper should revolve around one core research problem; multiple analytical questions should usually serve that problem rather than compete with it.
3. Before claiming novelty, know the important scholars, foundational work, recent frontier, and closest papers in the field.
4. A practical puzzle is acceptable only after identifying how scholarship conceptualizes and studies it.
5. New data, AI, large-scale text, or statistical methods are leverage for answering a known research difficulty; they are not a substitute for the research question.
6. The research object, theoretical construct, variable, and data source must refer to the same phenomenon. Clarify what a document or proxy does and does not represent.
7. A good study makes its theoretical logic, variables, data, empirical process, and robustness understandable and credible.
8. Findings must be compared with prior findings so that the paper produces a clear update in knowledge.
9. Do not keep analyses merely because they took time to produce. Reuse them only when they serve the paper.
10. A feasible research strategy is often to understand one excellent paper deeply and add a limited but real improvement through data, theory, method, identification, measurement, or context.
